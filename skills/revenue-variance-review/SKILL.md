Runs monthly and quarterly, after billing closes, for commercial planning, performance, trading and finance teams.

Produces a chart mapping revenue, margin, forecast and variance together, with KPIs and commentary.

## Establish the inputs

Ask for what you need and take it however the user has it — typed figures, pasted text, an uploaded file, or a live connection. Do not require a format. Name what you need in plain commercial terms and let the user map their columns once.

Required:

- **Billed revenue actuals** for the period, by product and segment.
- **Current year forecast** for the same period. Forecast files often span three years, so select the relevant period rather than assuming the file covers one year.

Wanted:

- **Prior year forecast**, for the year-on-year comparison.
- **Margin after all costs and deductions** against revenue. The user may have their own name for this figure.
- **Sold versus in-service position**, to size work in progress. If only orders and billed revenue are available, the difference approximates it — say so if you use that approximation.
- **Open pipeline and cover**, for the forward view.

## Ask before analysing

- **Which cut** — product mix or segment. Default: segment.
- **Period** — month or quarter. Default: month.
- **Commentary.** Ask the user for theirs. If they have none, write one and label it as drafted rather than supplied.

## Locate the gap

Size the variance against current year forecast, then against prior year. Do the same for margin. Revenue leads; margin qualifies it — landing revenue on forecast while margin falls is a worse month than the revenue line suggests.

## Attribute it

Attribute the gap against causes that can be checked in the data, not derived from it:

- **Slippage** — expected billing dates moved out of the period.
- **Late billing** — delivered but not yet billed.
- **Work in progress** — sold but not yet in service, so not yet billable.
- **Thin opportunity cover** — insufficient pipeline behind the period to have supported the forecast.
- **Seasonal buying behaviour** — known industry patterns, such as public sector spend loading into the end of the financial year.

Price, volume and mix effects are already resolved upstream before opportunities are recorded, and discounts are priced in. Do not attempt to decompose the gap into them.

## State what is left to cover

Close on the forward position: the remaining gap to forecast for the period or year, and whether open pipeline and cover support closing it. This is the part a reader can act on.

## What good looks like

The gap is quantified, located to a cause, and the forward position is stated. A weak review narrates the shortfall at length and stops there, which tells the reader nothing they didn't already know from the number.

Where the data does not support attributing a gap, say so and ask the user for what is missing. An unattributed variance is a legitimate finding and usually means an input is incomplete. Reaching for a plausible cause is the failure mode to avoid — it survives the meeting and misdirects the response for a month.

Margin moving independently of revenue is the signal most often missed. Check it every time, even when revenue lands.

## Rules

- MUST state the period, the comparison basis, and the as-of date of every input.
- MUST say when a gap cannot be attributed, and prompt for the missing data.
- MUST label commentary as drafted when the user did not supply it.
- MUST report margin alongside revenue, not only when revenue moves.
- NEVER decompose a variance into price, volume or mix effects.
- NEVER attribute a gap to a cause the data does not evidence.
- NEVER fabricate forecast, actuals or margin figures.
